01 How it starts

It starts with a crack.
Water does the rest.

A hairline crack lets water into the layers under the surface. Every wheel that rolls over it pumps that water through weakened road. The surface breaks up, loose material is thrown out, and the hole grows.

Caught at stage 1, it is a routine repair. Missed until stage 4, it is a crash risk.

02 What it costs

Then it becomes a crash.
And the toll keeps rising.

Pothole-related deaths have risen every year since 2021. In 2024 alone, 2,385 people were killed in crashes the official record attributes to potholes.1

Up from 1,555 in 2020. Official figures count recorded crashes only — crashes that were never reported are not in these numbers.

In proportion

Potholes account for about 1.2% of India’s road deaths — 2,161 of 1,72,890 in 2023.2,3 A small share, but a category that is, by definition, a maintenance failure. Every one of these crashes happened on a road that some authority is responsible for keeping in repair.

03 Why it stays broken

When everyone owns the road,
no one owns the pothole.

India’s roads are split between national, state, city and village authorities. Before a citizen can complain, they have to work out who is responsible — and each authority has its own channel. Rarely is there a public record of what was reported, who is accountable and when it will be fixed.

One journey. Up to four owners.

The pothole is the symptom. The missing owner is the problem.
